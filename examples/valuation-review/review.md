# Doruk Filtrasyon — valuation review

Reviewing: *Project Filtre — Valuation Materials, v11*, 12 March 2026, DCF section.
Valuation date: 31 December 2025. Currency EUR m. All figures illustrative — see
`model-as-received.md`.

## Finding

Enterprise value falls from 1,193.3 to 799.0 and equity value from 1,013.3 to 619.0, a
cut of 33%, once the terminal block is made internally consistent and the discount rate
carries a country risk premium. The revenue growth argument the committee spent its first
session on is worth 0.9% of enterprise value if it is an argument about timing, and 12.0%
only if it changes the FY2030 revenue level — a distinction nobody in the room had drawn.

At 81.5% this is a valuation of a terminal assumption with a five-year forecast attached.
Under the corrected structure the growth component collapses to 30.8 of a terminal value
of 878.4 — 3.5% — because terminal ROIC of 11% against a 9.5% WACC leaves a 150bp spread,

in the perpetual growth rate the adviser chose.

## Findings

**C1 — the terminal value assumes growth with no capital behind it. Correction.**
Terminal capex is set equal to D&A and working capital is held flat, so terminal
reinvestment is zero while g is 2.5%. That is an infinite incremental return on capital.
The tell is in the model's own output: FCFF steps from 66.4 in FY2030 to 80.9 in the
terminal year, a 21.8% jump at exactly the boundary where the forecast stops being
visible, with no operational event behind it. Applying `TV = NOPAT_{N+1}(1 − g/ROIC)/(w − g)`
at a terminal ROIC of 11% requires net reinvestment of 14.6 per year, against the 12.6
FY2030 already runs. ΔEV −221.2.

**C2 — the discount rate has no country risk premium. Correction, then judgement.**
The build-up uses a euro risk-free rate and a developed-market equity risk premium, then
applies the result to cash flows generated by Turkish operating assets. The euro
denomination of the forecast does not remove the exposure; it relocates it. The premium
must be sourced for the valuation date from Damodaran's country risk premium dataset
(`pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/`, retrieved and dated) or built from the sovereign
CDS spread, and the ERP itself cross-checked against Kroll's recommended figure and the
Fernandez/IESE survey for the same year. *[illustrative: +110bp central, band 0 to +180bp
across those sources.]* ΔEV −174.2 at 9.5%.

**C3 — terminal growth of 2.5% is above the ceiling the currency allows. Judgement.**
Perpetual growth cannot exceed the long-run nominal growth of the economy the cash flows
are denominated in, which is why Damodaran caps stable growth at the risk-free rate
(*Investment Valuation*, 3rd edition, Wiley, 2012). Against euro long rates at the
valuation date *[illustrative — retrieve the 10-year bund or euro swap rate for 31
December 2025]*, 2.5% is at or through the cap. ΔEV −79.2 at 2.0%.

**C4 — the mid-year adjustment is applied twice to the terminal value. Correction.**
The model uplifts `TV_N` by (1+w)^0.5 and then discounts it at (1+w)^-4.5. Those are two
ways of writing the same half-year shift, not two shifts. Doing both overstates the
terminal value by (1.085)^0.5 − 1 = 4.16%. ΔEV −38.9. The explicit-period treatment is
correct and stays.

**J1 — the growth argument, priced.** Judgement. Two versions, and they are worth
different money. If the deal team is arguing about *timing* — the same FY2030 revenue of
779.7 reached on a slower path — it is worth ΔEV −10.4, or 0.9%. If it is arguing that
the FY2030 *level* is wrong, and 8/8/7/6/6 takes FY2030 revenue to 673.1 from 779.7, it flows through
the terminal value and is worth ΔEV −143.6, or 12.0%. The adviser's contracted-platform
evidence bears on the first version and not the second.

**J2 — the flat 18% EBITDA margin is an assumption, not a plan.** Judgement. Held constant
for five years and into perpetuity through a period of tariff and input-cost movement. One
point of margin is ΔEV −92.1, or 7.7% — more than the timing argument by a factor of nine,
and it was not discussed at all.

The four corrections taken individually sum to −513.5, which would put EV at 679.8.

growth rate makes the reinvestment correction cheaper, and a higher discount rate makes
both cheaper still. Sensitivities are not additive and the table above must not be read as
a bridge.

## What would settle this

1. **Terminal ROIC.** Doruk's invested capital and NOPAT for FY2021–FY2025, on a
   consistent definition, with leases capitalised. One page. It settles a 22.7% question.
2. **Country risk premium.** The retrieval, dated, from one of the three published
   sources, plus a decision on whether it applies at company or currency level. It settles
   a 23.6% question and is an hour's work.
3. **The growth argument, restated.** Ask the deal team and the adviser the same question:
   does your case change FY2030 revenue, or only the path to it? Their answer is worth
   either 0.9% or 12.0%, and until it is given the first committee session cannot be
   priced at all.
4. **Margin.** The FY2021–25 realised EBITDA margin range and what drove the movement.
   Worth more than the argument already had.

Items not raised: the working capital ratio, the tax rate, the D&A convention, the share
count. Each was checked; none moves enterprise value by as much as 1%.
